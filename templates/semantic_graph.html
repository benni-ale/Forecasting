Semantic Diffusion Graph

Work in progress
This page is under construction. It will host the interactive visualisation of the semantic-diffusion model at the core of the research project. Below is what it will show and how it works.
The idea

News relevance for a stock is, in part, relevant to semantically related stocks too. The model treats informational relevance as a field that both decays over time and diffuses across a graph of companies, but only among companies in the same industry — the discrete heat equation

dR/dt = −( D·L + λI ) R ,    R(t) = e−λt e−DtL R(0)

where L = diag(di) − W is the graph Laplacian, λ the speed of forgetting (half-life), and D the semantic permeability of the market. With D = 0 the model collapses to the time-decay KPI already in production — a built-in sanity check.

How the graph is built
  1. Industry gate. Candidate edges exist only between companies assigned to the same industry; companies without an industry are excluded.
  2. Similarity. Within each industry, cosine similarity between the OpenAI embeddings of company descriptions (already cached in company_correlation_matrix).
  3. Sparsify. Keep each node's top-k neighbours (k-NN) or a threshold τ; clamp negatives to 0.
  4. Symmetrise. W = ½(A + A⊤) so the graph is undirected.
  5. Laplacian. L = diag(row sums) − W (unnormalised, to keep exact mass conservation).
  6. Diffuse. One implicit-Euler step per day evolves (R, V=S·R); the sparse solve is cheap for a few hundred tickers.
What you'll see here
  • Interactive company similarity graph (nodes = tickers, edges = strong same-industry semantic links).
  • Diffusion demo: inject a relevance pulse on one ticker and watch it spread to neighbours day by day.
  • Heatmaps of the similarity matrix W and Laplacian L.
  • Controls for k, threshold, D and the half-life, with a live D = 0 toggle.
  • Feeds the ticker_cross_diffused_sentiment table consumed by the dashboard KPI.

The mathematical formulation follows the graph diffusion kernel (Kondor & Lafferty, 2002), diffusion maps (Coifman & Lafon, 2006), and connects to graph neural diffusion (GRAND, Chamberlain et al., 2021) — the interpretable counterpart of a learned GNN.
